Colophon

File layout. Each session has a folder. Inside it, one file per person: week-01/artist_1.html through week-01/artist_24.html, plus artist_1.pdf and artist_1.txt alongside. The final session lives in final/. Files are exported straight out of The Comet and committed without renaming.

The Comet. One issue per reading week, sitting at the repository root as comet_1.html through comet_13.html. Issue numbers follow week numbers, so there is no issue 4, 8, or 12: those are proof days, which carry no reading and no response tool. Ten issues in all, each the same tool with a different item list and a different colour scheme.

Every issue is named after a real science fiction fanzine. Not decoration. The zine this course produces belongs to a lineage that began in 1930 with The Comet, mimeographed by the Science Correspondence Club in Chicago, and the names are a way of keeping that visible on the page students open every week. A few are chosen for fit rather than chronology. Slant sits on the permacomputing week because Walt Willis hand-set and printed it on a press in his own house in Belfast. Quandry sits on the measurement week partly for its name and partly because Lee Hoffman edited it under a gender-neutral byline and revealed herself as its editor at Worldcon in 1951. Cheap Truth sits on the week where fiction tests the method, because Bruce Sterling published it uncopyrighted, on one double-sided page, explicitly inviting xerox pirates to reprint it, and used it to attack the state of the field. Hyphen closes the reading weeks because it is named after a punctuation mark and that week is about form.

To put real names on the buttons, edit the STUDENTS list at the top of this file's script. Filenames never change, so nothing breaks when a name is added, corrected, or removed. Leave an entry empty and the button shows its number alone.

To change a session, edit the SESSIONS list in the same place.

Colour. Every session carries its own scheme, set in the theme field of its entry: four values, ink, accent, second, and paper, plus an optional onAccent for badge text when the accent runs light. Each palette is lifted from a real cover, named under the heading of the card it belongs to.

Each card is the palette of its own issue. Week 1 uses the exact variables from comet_1.html: aubergine, lime, deep teal, on pale lime. Open the card and the issue side by side and they are the same object. The cards for issues not yet built are the specification for them: when Comet No. 2 is made, it takes its --cobalt, --chrome and --pulpred from the Week 2 card, not the other way round.

The inks and accents are taken from the covers named on each card; the papers are not. Every stock in this set is a cool neutral, tinted only toward its own week's hue, because a page of seventeen cream cards reads as one beige field rather than seventeen distinct sessions. Weeks 1, 3 and 5 keep the navy and pulp red of Amazing Stories and a mid-century rocket plate, over blue-whites. Week 6 keeps the crimson of an Ace Vintage Science Fiction Month cover on pale rose, which suits a week about extraction and its accounting. Weeks 2 and 10 come from a Japanese science magazine for children, Tokyo, November 1949: deep teal and a red-orange bus, over mint. Week 8 takes the violet of a flying-saucer cover. Week 11 is Space Cat on Mushrooms, brick red over clay. The proof days and Week 14 come from NoPattern Studio's Eternal Reference, hot pink and magenta over plum and deep green. Weeks 7, 9, 13 and 15 come from contemporary zine spreads by Luana Lloyd: forest and terracotta, cobalt and hot pink, dusty rose.

Every combination was checked on four pairs: the week number against its block, badge text against accent, chip text on hover, and body text on paper. Nothing sits below a ratio of 3.
